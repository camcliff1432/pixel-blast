## Duel mode (`fairPieces`)

Duel runs draw pieces from a seeded sequence instead of re-reading the live picture, so two players
on one seed get the same trays at the same move numbers. The color weights are the ones the normal
pick would use on the untouched picture, frozen there. That removes the help the adaptive pick gives
you late in a level, and it is not free:

```
Pixel Blast balance · 100 runs/level · think 1.2s · overrides {"fairPieces":true}

level       size   pixels  win   win+3cont  cont used 0/1/2/3   med time  med moves
heart       10x10  62        72%       99%     72/21/5/1         25s       22
smiley      10x10  78        48%       98%     48/30/14/6        36s       31
fish        10x10  60        50%       97%     50/31/12/4        35s       30
tree        10x10  64        52%       99%     52/27/14/6        35s       30
mushroom    12x12  96        35%       88%     35/23/25/5        51s       43
landscape   10x10  100       32%       95%     32/43/19/1        48s       41
rocket      12x12  144       28%       89%     28/33/14/14       60s       51
```

Mean first-try win: **59% adaptive → 45% fair**. Heart barely moves (74 → 72) because it is almost
one color; the multi-color pictures lose the most.

`colorNeedBonus` is the one knob that pushes back, and not far. It sets how much the weighting
flattens toward rare colors — Heart's two yellow pixels are the binding constraint, not its sixty
red ones, so flatter is better up to a point (100 runs/level, so ±5 points of noise):

| `colorNeedBonus` | 0 | 1 | 2 | **4 (current)** | 8 | 16 | 32 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mean first-try win | 38% | 43% | 42% | **45%** | 47% | 48% | 42% |

Flat from 4 to 16 within the noise, falling off either side. Not worth a Duel-only override; close
the rest of the gap with level design instead.

The same flag makes a run exactly replayable from its seed and move list, which is what the
regression test in [COCOS_PORT.md](COCOS_PORT.md) is built on, and what a Daily Puzzle would need.

## Read-outs and next tuning steps
- **Level 1 is too hard for a first level.** Heart is a 74% first-try win; a tutorial level should be about 95%. Options: a smaller picture, per-level easier settings (bigger combo bonus, longer belt), or a guided first level.
- **The curve is flat.** All seven levels sit between 41% and 74%, drifting down with pixel count rather than following a designed shape. Plan for a sawtooth: easy, easy, medium, then a "hard" level every 5th or so.
- **Continues are now a real decision.** A wallet that stays flat (42 → 59), about 8 continues bought per 23 levels, and in about a quarter of jams the player wants one but can't afford it. Those are the moments for a rewarded ad ("watch an ad to continue") once ads exist.
- **Caveat:** the bot isn't a human. Real players place slower, misjudge colors, and sometimes play better. Validate with real play-tests before locking numbers.

## Big pictures (added with 12 colors and up to 32×32)

Large pictures were nearly unwinnable at first: a 1024-pixel, 8-color sunset gave the bot a 0–8% first-try win. Adding more ammo barely helped. The bottleneck was belt throughput: more placements meant more chances to jam. The fix is automatic scaling, and pictures up to 12×12 are unchanged:

| Picture size | Shots per block | Belt capacity | Lap time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| up to 12×12 / under 150 px | 1 | 5 | 5.7 s |
| 16×16 (256 px) | 3 | 6 | 5.4 s |
| 24×24 (576 px) | 6 | 9 | 4.7 s |
| 32×32 (1024 px) | 10 | 10 | 4.0 s |

Bot results on the test sunset after scaling (12 runs each): 16×16 58%, 24×24 50%, 32×32 42% first-try wins, and 100% with continues, at about 45–60 s. Sand mode is about 0–8 points harder.

Same-color shooters now merge in the queue. Win rates are unchanged, and lost shots dropped to zero.

## Boss and Sandstorm (node sim/modes.js)

- **Boss:** a regrow interval of 3.5 s × √(60 / pixels) (Heart 3.4 s, Rocket 2.3 s) plays about 10–20 points harder than Classic. Bot first-try wins: Heart 67%, Smiley 50%, Fish 73%, Tree 63%, Mushroom 33%, Landscape 37%, Rocket 33%.
- **Sandstorm:** at first the bot always lost to board jams before the storm mattered, so in Sandstorm a jam triggers a 6-pixel surge instead of ending the run. After that the bot survived forever, because it out-cleared the storm's top speed. The current settings (start 2.0 s, ×0.99 per pixel, floor 0.2 s) give a median survival of about 3.5 minutes, and every run ends buried.
